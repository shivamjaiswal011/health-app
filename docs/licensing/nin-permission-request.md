# Permission request — ICMR-NIN (IFCT 2017)

**Subject:** Permission request — IFCT 2017 proximate data in a free, open-source nutrition app

Dear Sir/Madam,

I am writing to request written permission to use a subset of the data published in
*Indian Food Composition Tables 2017* (Longvah, T., Ananthan, R., Bhaskarachary, K. and
Venkaiah, K., National Institute of Nutrition, ICMR, Hyderabad) in a non-commercial,
open-source mobile application.

**What the application is**

[APP NAME] is a personal health-tracking app for exercise and diet, which I am
developing as a free and open-source project ([REPOSITORY URL]). It is not sold, carries
no advertising, no subscription and no in-app purchases, and collects no user data: all
information stays on the user's own device, and there is no server. Its purpose is to
let individuals in India log their meals and training and see their own progress over
time.

**What I am requesting**

Permission to store, and to distribute as part of the application, the **proximate
principles** for the 528 foods in IFCT 2017 — that is, energy, protein, total fat,
available carbohydrate and dietary fibre, expressed per 100 g. I am not requesting the
vitamin, mineral, amino acid, fatty acid, polyphenol or other detailed component tables.

I am aware of the notice on the copyright page, which permits reproduction for personal
use with acknowledgment but requires prior written permission before the data may be
stored or reproduced in electronic format for creating a product. That is why I am
writing rather than proceeding.

**How the data would be attributed**

Each food record would carry IFCT 2017 as its source, visible to the user. The
application's About screen and its public repository would both cite the tables in the
form given in the publication:

> Longvah, T., Ananthan, R., Bhaskarachary, K. and Venkaiah, K. (2017). Indian Food
> Composition Tables 2017, National Institute of Nutrition, Indian Council of Medical
> Research, Hyderabad, Telangana, India.

I would be glad to accept any conditions the Institute wishes to attach — a specific
attribution wording, a restriction on the fields used, a requirement that the data not
be redistributed in bulk or in machine-readable form separately from the application, or
periodic confirmation of continued non-commercial status. I am equally willing to adjust
the scope of the request if a narrower subset would be more appropriate.

Existing Indian nutrition applications rely largely on crowd-sourced values of uncertain
quality, and the difference the Institute's measured data would make to the accuracy of
what Indian users see is considerable. I would be grateful for your consideration.

Thank you for your time.
