Dear Professor Jaacks,

Thank you for publishing the Indian Nutrient Databank. The combination of raw foods,
prepared recipes and serving sizes fills a gap that no other openly available Indian
dataset does, and the retention-factor work behind the recipes is exactly what makes it
usable for real meal logging rather than only for research.

I am building [APP NAME], a free and open-source diet and training tracker for
individual use ([REPOSITORY URL]). It is not sold, carries no advertising, and stores
everything on the user's own device with no server. I would like to include INDB as its
Indian food database, with attribution.

I have two questions before doing so.

**1. Could the repository carry an explicit licence file?**

The paper states that it is published under CC BY 4.0, but the
`Indian-Nutrient-Databank-INDB-` repository does not contain a LICENSE file, so the
terms covering the data files themselves are ambiguous. Adding one — CC BY 4.0, or
whatever you consider correct for the data as distinct from the article — would settle
it, and would help everyone else who finds the repository with the same question.

**2. What is the position on the underlying ICMR-NIN data?**

The copyright page of IFCT 2017 requires prior written permission before its data may be
stored in electronic format for creating a product. Since INDB's Stage 1 values derive
substantially from IFCT 2017 and 2004, I wanted to ask whether you obtained permission
from ICMR-NIN, or whether you took the view that composition values are factual data not
subject to that restriction. I ask genuinely rather than to make a point: I have a
permission request pending with NIN myself, and knowing how you approached it would help
me get this right.

I am happy to attribute INDB in whatever form you prefer, in both the application and its
repository, and to cite the paper alongside it.

Thank you for making the work available.
